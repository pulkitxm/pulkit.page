01 / The context

The real problem
comes first.

Kaksha began with my mother, a government-school teacher, planning an entire timetable by hand. One absent teacher could mean rebuilding part of the day before the first bell. The useful question was not how to generate a schedule. It was how to make her existing process easier to trust.

This visual study gives that real product a calmer interface: a clear grid, colors that help with scanning, and a small set of familiar destinations. The product story is real. The interface compositions on this page are a new design direction, using invented sample data.

01 / Design decision

Keep the grid central.

The timetable is the work. Navigation stays compact, leaving the schedule space to breathe.

02 / Design decision

Make mistakes visible.

Clash detection belongs alongside the schedule, close to the decision that caused it.

03 / Design decision

Meet the teacher where she is.

The original product runs on an Android tablet and works offline, because the staff room does not always have reliable connectivity.
